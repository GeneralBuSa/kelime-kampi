The idea did not begin as a fashion: galaxies rotate so fast that the stars on their edges should fly apart if only the visible mass were holding them together. Extra unseen mass would supply the missing gravity. Alternative theories try to change the laws of gravity instead of adding matter, but they have so far had difficulty explaining a wide range of observations at once, from galaxy clusters to the pattern of the early universe. Dark matter has not been detected in a laboratory, which leaves room for doubt. Still, it remains the simplest account of several independent facts, which is why most cosmologists treat it as a working hypothesis rather than as a myth.

## r23|reading|B2|Sosyal bilimler
P: A national census is more than a headcount. Governments use it to decide where to build schools, how to draw electoral districts and how to distribute funds. Because the results affect political power, every census faces disputes about who is counted and how questions are asked. Some groups are harder to reach: people without a fixed address, recent migrants, or households that distrust official forms. If they are missed, the map of need becomes distorted and services go to the wrong places. Modern censuses therefore combine traditional door-to-door visits with administrative records and online forms. The aim is not perfect knowledge of every person, which is impossible, but a picture that is fair enough to guide public decisions.

## r24|reading|C1|Arkeoloji
P: Archaeology once concentrated on palaces, tombs and the objects of elites, partly because those remains survive more visibly and attract funding. In recent decades the field has widened. Soil samples, animal bones and the layout of ordinary houses now tell researchers how most people ate, worked and died. This shift does not make royal inscriptions useless; it places them in a broader economy of labour and food. Climate data from tree rings and ice cores can even show whether a harvest failed in the years before a city was abandoned. The result is a less romantic but more accurate past, in which collapse is rarely the work of a single battle and more often the slow outcome of drought, trade disruption and social tension arriving together.

## r25|reading|B2|Hukuk
P: Copyright law tries to balance two aims: giving creators a period in which they can earn money from their work, and allowing the rest of society to use ideas once that period ends. Works that have entered the public domain can be copied, translated and taught without asking permission. This is why older novels can appear in cheap school editions. The length of protection has grown over time, which some economists see as a gift to large publishers rather than to living authors. Others reply that longer terms encourage investment in restoring old films and recordings. Whatever the right number of years may be, the public domain is not a legal accident; it is the part of culture that the law deliberately returns to everyone.
